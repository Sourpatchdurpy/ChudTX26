## Prize-track limits (organizer announcement, quoted)
> "You are limited to opting into 3 non-MLH prize tracks. This means you can only attempt to win three prize tracks from either HackTX (aside from Best Overall, Best Novice, and Best Pitch, as everyone is running for Best Overall, we will check teams if they qualify for Best Novice, and Best Pitching applies for teams that participate in the pitching competition tomorrow) or our partners.
> However, you ARE allowed to enter into as many MLH prize tracks that you qualify for (any prize track marked [MLH])."

**Our selection:**
- **Non-MLH:** Meta "Bringing People Closer Together with AI", and Most Entertaining (2 of 3; the third slot is intentionally unused)
- **Automatic or free:** Best Overall, and Best Pitch (Discord signup)
- **MLH:** Best Use of Vultr, Best .Tech Domain Name

## General rules (Devpost)
- **Build window:** 11:30am CT Sat Oct 24 to 11:30am CT Sun Oct 25, 2026. All code must be written during the hacking period.
- **Teams:** 1–4 people. Team members must be present in person for judging.
- **Submission:** on Devpost, with a demo video, by the deadline. Soft submission is due Saturday 11:30pm.
- **Eligibility:** ages 18+, college students, US participants.
- **Judging:** 2–5pm Sunday in WEL. Pitching is 5:30–6pm Sunday.
- **Devpost judging criteria:**
  - **Technology:** how technically impressive or difficult the hack is.
  - **Design:** UX and interface quality.
  - **Completion:** whether it works and achieves its goals in the time limit.
  - **Learning:** whether the team stretched its skills.

## Meta: "Bringing People Closer Together with AI" (from the official sheet)
> "At Meta, we believe technology can bring people closer together and deepen real human connections. Build the AI-powered social product you wish existed, or one that helps strengthen connections you care about in new ways.
> AI should play a meaningful role in the experience. It might synthesize a group discussion into plans everyone would enjoy, organize scattered family updates into a shared story, or identify common interests that could spark a new connection."

**Rubric:**

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| How meaningfully it strengthens human connection | 30% |
| How essential and well-integrated AI is | 25% |
| Originality | 20% |
| Technical execution / working demo | 20% |

**Prize:** the top 3 teams (selected by Meta judges) get an invitation to the Round 2 Hackathon at Meta's Menlo Park campus (travel and lodging provided, subject to eligibility and the Round 2 rules), plus a Meta swag bag.

**Tools:** any stack is allowed. Meta's AI tools are encouraged, not required: Muse Spark (text), Muse Image, Muse Voice Transcribe, and Muse (agent; connectors). Each participant can redeem $50 in Meta API credits via the intake form QR code on the sheet.

**What to submit:** a 2–3 minute demo video, suggested breakdown:
1. What problem it solves and how it brings people closer together with AI (~30s)
2. A working product demo of key features (~90s)
3. How AI plays a role and is integrated (~30s)
4. The tools, harnesses and models used, and how they helped the build (~30s)

**Feedback from Meta employees at their table** (Saturday evening, paraphrased):
- **On the hangout ending:** "Suggesting an in-person hangout to a lobby of strangers after an online game felt disconnected." We cut it.
- **On what counts as connection:** "The product must make a noticeable difference in interpersonal interaction or communication *after* it's used." The example given was Meta glasses translating between two people who couldn't otherwise talk. This shaped our collaboration round and partner cards.
- **A third employee** reviewed the current concept and raised no concerns.
